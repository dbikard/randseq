Counts to log2 fold change

Each plasmid’s depletion is the log2 ratio of its share of the recipient sample to its share of the donor library, normalised so that the sample’s median plasmid sits at 0. Four details matter:

  • The pseudocount is added to raw counts, before normalisation: (n+1)/Σ(n+1) per sample, then the ratio. Normalised counts are far below 1, so adding 1 after normalising would flatten every ratio towards 1.
  • Shares are over the plasmids that pass the count filter, not over every molecule sequenced. That is a per-sample constant: it shifts all log2FC of a sample by the same amount, and the median normalisation below removes it.
  • Each sample is normalised to its median plasmid (normalize="median", the default): each column’s median log2FC is subtracted, so the median plasmid has log2FC 0, a fold change of 1. This is median-ratio normalisation, as in DESeq2; on the log2 scale, subtracting the median is the same as rescaling the counts by the median ratio. Shares alone are not enough: when a few plasmids take a large share of a sample’s reads, every other plasmid’s share shrinks and its log2FC falls below 0, by an amount that differs from strain to strain. Against the median plasmid, a depletion threshold of −0.5 means the same in every strain. normalize="total" stops at the shares.
  • The filter is reference count > count_threshold (default 20), in the one reference column.

count_threshold filters on donor abundance, not on depletion, so raising it costs power but does not choose which motifs are called. It does set how many plasmids carry each motif, so num_sequences is comparable between experiments only at the same threshold.

Choosing which strand to report a motif on

A recognition site and its reverse complement are the same site. The library is scanned on both strands, so a motif and its reverse complement always get identical statistics — same number of supporting sequences, same depletion score, same average log2FC. They are one answer, and which spelling to report is a tie that has to be broken the same way every time, or two result tables cannot be compared (GAGACC in one, GGTCTC in the other).

canonical_motif reports the strand REBASE uses, so results line up with the literature and with enzyme names (GGTCTC is BsaI/Eco31I). For motifs REBASE does not know, which includes most of what a discovery screen finds, it falls back to the alphabetically first strand: arbitrary, but the same on every run and machine. A motif REBASE lists on both strands takes the alphabetically first too.

The search space

A pattern (d1, spacer, d2) is the shape of a site: defined bases, undefined gap, defined bases. get_patterns returns the shapes the scan searches by default, each in the one form the scan uses.

The default is 43 patterns: contiguous sites of 5–7 bases, and gapped sites whose halves are 2–4 bases, with 5–8 defined bases in all and a gap of 1–8. It fits 837 of the 983 distinct motifs in the REBASE Gold Standard set shipped with the package. The arguments widen it: get_patterns(contiguous=(4, 8), halves=(2, 5), max_spacer=9) (68 patterns) fits 902, and adding max_defined=9 (77) fits 911, at about 8 times the motifs tested (3.6 million against 460,000 on the larger example library). That is not the default because, at the default thresholds, a wider search adds one-base-longer descriptions of weak or degenerate sites that steps 3 and 4 do not yet catch.

A site whose half has an undefined base inside it, such as CNGTANNNNNNTTC, has two gaps, and no pattern describes it: 47 of the 983 Gold Standard motifs are of this kind, almost all Type I. Each concrete variant of such a site has a one-gap shape, here (5, 6, 3), which can be passed in patterns to find the site. The variants are then reported as separate rows (CAGTANNNNNNTTC, CCGTANNNNNNTTC, …), because writing N inside a half would give a shape the scan does not search.

Presence and log2FC lookups for a hand-picked list of motifs, independent of find_restricted_motifs. A motif may use IUPAC codes (N, R, Y, …) and regex character classes ([AG]) interchangeably, and is searched on both strands of the insert with its context. Is a motif’s depletion more than the sample’s own noise?

The scan in 00_core tests a great many motifs (about 330,000 with the default 5 or more supporting plasmids on the smaller example library, 460,000 on the larger), so some will look perfect by luck alone. How many supporting plasmids a call needs before that stops being a plausible explanation depends on the sample, not on a constant.

Under the null, a motif’s depleted count is Binomial(n, p) where p is the rate at which its carriers would deplete in this sample if no site were involved: their background. A noisy sample has a higher p and therefore needs more evidence for the same claim, automatically. How p is estimated for each motif is the next section; first, the binomial form itself.

Shuffled data bear this out. With each sample’s log2FC shuffled across its plasmids, 20 times for each of six strains of the two example libraries, find_restricted_motifs at the defaults calls nothing, at any min_support from 2 to 6; the smallest q in any shuffle is 0.005, on a row the confidence bound rejects.

What this does not model. The binomial treats the plasmids carrying a motif as independent draws. They are not: motifs co-occur, which is the whole subject of step 4 of the core pipeline. So the p-value is a screen against chance, not a complete model of how a spurious call arises: a shadow of a real site is highly significant by this test and still needs explaining away.

# BH: p*n/rank is 0.04 for all four here, so all four q-values are 0.04.
_benjamini_hochberg([0.01, 0.02, 0.03, 0.04])
array([0.04, 0.04, 0.04, 0.04])

The background depends on the insert’s GC content

Within one sample, the chance that a plasmid looks depleted is not the same for every insert. On the 20 nt library, AT-rich inserts deplete far more often than GC-rich ones in some strains, for no reason a site could explain (possibly a fitness cost of such inserts; the cause is unknown). Fraction of plasmids below −0.5, on log2FC as calculate_log2fc returns them:

_c = pd.read_csv(os.path.join(get_example_data_dir(), "countsTable.csv.gz"), index_col=0)
_l2 = calculate_log2fc(_c[["MFDpir", "E1114_T0", "H120_T0", "JJ1886_T0"]], reference_column="MFDpir")
_gc = pd.Series([(s.count("G") + s.count("C")) / len(s) for s in _l2.index], index=_l2.index)
_bins = pd.cut(_gc, [0, .25, .35, .45, .55, .65, 1], include_lowest=True)
_by_gc = (_l2 < -0.5).groupby(_bins, observed=True).mean().T.round(2)
_by_gc.loc["(plasmids)"] = _bins.value_counts().reindex(_by_gc.columns).values

E1114 depletes 0.58 of the inserts with GC ≤ 25% and 0.07 of those above 65%; H120 0.46 against 0.02. JJ1886 hardly varies. Against one sample-wide rate, any motif made of A and T inherits the excess of the inserts that carry it and is called: 42 calls in E1114 and 39 in H120, almost all AT-rich.

The fix is the standard one in motif enrichment (HOMER’s GC-matched background): compare a motif’s carriers with plasmids of the same GC content. plasmid_background gives each plasmid the depletion rate of the plasmids in its insert-GC decile, and a motif’s null rate is the mean of its carriers’ backgrounds. GC is counted on the insert alone: the flanks are the same in every plasmid. The bins are deciles, fixed rather than a parameter: fine enough to follow the trend above, coarse enough that every rate rests on hundreds of plasmids. A 20 nt insert has only 21 possible GC contents, so deciles that would share an edge merge: the 20 nt library has 8 bins of 660–2,020 plasmids, the 150 nt panel library 10 of 1,805–3,858.

Merging motifs by spelling

find_iupac_equivalent_motifs groups same-length motifs that differ at ≤ max_mismatches positions (N positions skipped, reverse complements considered) and merges each group into one IUPAC motif, the union of bases at each position. It works on spelling alone, on any results table — across strains, say. Within one sample find_restricted_motifs already merges a degenerate site on the data, a variant joining only when its own carriers deplete like the site’s.

Groups are connected components: A~B and B~C merge all three, even when A and C are further apart. A motif that matched through its reverse complement is flipped before merging, and the merged motif is spelled on the strand of the group’s first row. Each row of the result, one per group with singletons included, gives merged_motif; original_motifs and original_motifs_oriented (as given, and on the merged strand); n_merged; max_pairwise_mismatches, over linked pairs; and rc_used, after sample_col when one is given. When df has them, num_sequences, avg_log2fc and fraction_depleted add total_sequences (their sum), avg_log2fc_weighted (weighted by num_sequences when present) and avg_fraction_depleted (unweighted mean).
